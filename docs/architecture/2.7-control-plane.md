## Ruled 2026-10-21: the broker

The execution model is a **pool of command-runner daemons**, one per account it stands for
([`6.2-process-pool.md`](6.2-process-pool.md)), and a **broker** that unifies eventing, telemetry and the command runner.
The owner, 2026-10-21: "we want a daemon that communicates locally via named pipes (I think) and remotely via REST (I
think). that's the new execution model"; then "I was thinking we had a pool of command runner daemons; not necessarily a
launchd daemon, though it might be"; "We might broker calls through the daemon"; and on the transport, "we'll stick with
http/2".

The broker is the one process a caller talks to. It:

1. **authenticates** the caller — the peer's uid from a Unix domain socket or a Windows named pipe locally, a token on
   the remote TLS leg;
2. **authorizes** the account the caller asks for, against the policy in its own profile ([§ Authorization at the
   socket](#authorization-at-the-socket)); the [`authz` provider](6.1-authorization.md) is the client side of this;
3. **selects** the runner standing for that account, or starts one — a plug point ([§ The runner
   selector](#the-runner-selector-a-plug-point));
4. **dispatches** the exec to it, and **forks** what comes back: operational events to the bus and the logger
   ([`2.8`](2.8-eventing-infrastructure.md)), narration and output to the caller's **console stream** ([§ Dispatch and
   the fork](#dispatch-and-the-fork));
5. **steers** the run on the control plane of this document, and **records** it: a per-run events file and a per-run
   console file, each with its own `seq`.

Two things the broker never does: it never runs a command, and it holds no interpreter. A runner does both
([`6.2`](6.2-process-pool.md)). And the broker is not always in the path: a caller may go **direct** to the runner
standing for its own account, with the same frames and no policy, because no grant is needed for one's own account.

**Ruled 2026-10-22, the day after.** Three things, each quoted where it lands. The broker is **unprivileged**: "we do
not want to be a key store. we need to rely on the OS for that." It holds no credential and mints no logon; the service
manager starts each runner as its account ([`6.2`](6.2-process-pool.md) § Ruled 2026-10-22), and the broker connects
to the runner's socket or pipe, or on Unix starts one with `sudo -u` under a sudoers rule when no service is
registered. § Open 1 is ruled for **the graph executor on the runner**, option 2: "The cost gives us what i think we
ultimately want: the ability to dispatch execution units to any device with a broker. The serialization boundary is
the cost we pay for that." Amended 2026-10-24: any device SSH reaches, with no broker installed there (§ The remote leg). And the unit that crosses is a control structure, `plan.logon`
([`2.3`](2.3-orchestration-primitives.md) § Ruled 2026-10-22): "a subgraph that is specifically designed to produce
(during planning) and execute a graph that runs in a local or remote logon." What the broker dispatches is therefore a
**posted graph** as well as a command, and § The remote leg below is the protocol for it.

**Ruled 2026-10-23 and 2026-10-24.** The owner ruled on the logon pipeline, on what travels on which line, on who elevates, and on how the broker remotes. Stated here; the body below is corrected to match, and the parts still open are in § Open.

1. **The logon pipeline.** A logon subgraph is the source of a three-tier request from the app, through the broker, to a subgraph-executor standing for an account. The app sends the account, the host when there is one, and the subgraph; the broker makes sure an executor is running as that account on that host, at the app's executor version, starting one with its own privileges or reusing one, and hands it the subgraph. The executor runs it the way the graph executor runs any graph, and runs many subgraphs in parallel. § The logon pipeline.
2. **The account belongs to the logon, and the broker elevates.** Every command inside a logon runs as its account. The broker runs under an account with elevation privileges; the app need not; automatic elevation is recommended, not required ([`6.2`](6.2-process-pool.md) § Configuring the broker's account). This amends the unprivileged broker of the ruling above; devlore still holds no credential.
3. **A processor is named by its host and account**, shown as `account@host`; the subgraph is its payload, not its identity.
4. **The three streams and their lines.** The executor's stderr is narration, which the broker streams back to the app as it is written, as bytes; the app writes it into its narration sink. Its stdout is the result, which the broker returns with the exit status when the run ends, as writ does in-process today: the executor narrates through `env.Status` while it runs, and the command emits its result once through `cli.Emit` when `Run` returns. Events travel on none of these lines: every component, the app, the broker and the executor, emits them through OpenTelemetry ([`2.8`](2.8-eventing-infrastructure.md)), and the broker's own are exec start and end, grants and refusals, and processors started and lost (#1169).
5. **The broker remotes through SSH.** No broker is installed on the machines devlore reaches. Over SSH the broker ships a bundle that ensures the executor is present, installing it if not, the subgraph is present, and the subgraph runs under the account's credentials. How a host is reached, its user, key, port and jump host, is the user's `~/.ssh/config`, which devlore does not own; the account SSH logs in with is not the logon's account, to which the broker on the far side elevates. § The remote leg.

**The transport is HTTP/2**, over the socket or pipe locally (h2c, cleartext with prior knowledge; see
[#689](https://github.com/NobleFactor/devlore-cli/issues/689)) and TLS remotely — the same API on both, so a client does
not know which it is on. Ruled against HTTP/3 the same day, for three reasons: the local leg cannot carry QUIC, which
is UDP and runs over neither a Unix socket nor a named pipe; Go has no standard-library HTTP/3 (the `x/net`
implementation is `internal`, not for production, and the standard-library work was shelved; the production option is
`quic-go`, a third-party stack); and the frame design below needs no cross-stream order, so HTTP/3's one relevant win,
no head-of-line blocking under loss, buys nothing a `seq`-numbered stream does not already have. The transport stays a
swappable layer; connection migration for a long remote run from a moving laptop is the case that would reopen it.

**What this changes in the document below.** § Framing gains narration as a wire channel and outputs as a fourth thing.
§ The wire becomes the broker's listener and gains the selector, the dispatch, the fork, the two streams, authorization
and the remote leg. § Message reference gains the exec request and the console frames. Everything else stands as
written on 2026-07-20 and 2026-07-21, including the Cassandra shape of the plane, the control-point, and the connection
model.

## Framing: three orthogonal pieces

A run is **steered** from outside and **observed** from outside — but "observed" is itself two distinct channels, so the
outward-facing surface is **three orthogonal subsystems**, not one:

1. **Control commands** — inbound. A consumer *steers* the run: `pause`, `stop`, `step`, served at the executor's
   control-point. This is the **control plane** proper, designed in the rest of this document.
2. **Event streams** — outbound, **structured**, machine-facing. The async event pipeline (`Event` +
   `SubscriptionManager`, ndjson, event categories) specified in
   [`6-execution-topology.md` §Telemetry](6-execution-topology.md#telemetry-asynchronous-event-pipeline); consumers
   subscribe and filter by execution identity. The app-agnostic bus, the hook interface that feeds it, and the
   OpenTelemetry mapping are designed in [`2.8-eventing-infrastructure.md`](2.8-eventing-infrastructure.md).
3. **Narration** — outbound, **human**. `status.Narrator`'s categorized terminal render (`Note` / `Warn` / `Succeed` /
   `Fail`), one per process, shared with the hosting application. **Revised 2026-10-21:** with a broker, the hosting
   application is on the other end of the wire, so narration **becomes a wire channel** — a frame tagged `narration`
   on the caller's console stream, rendered by the *caller's* `Narrator`. It stays orthogonal to events: a distinct
   tag, not a projection of them. This is the one change the broker makes to the framing of 2026-07-21.

A fourth thing crosses the wire that is none of the three: **outputs**, a command's stdout, which is a *result*, not an
event. It travels on the console stream beside narration ([§ The two streams](#the-two-streams-events-and-console)),
and `result.Pipeline` remains its in-process channel.

**Orthogonal** means none is downstream of another: narration does **not** consume the event stream, and commands do
**not** feed it. They share exactly one thing — the **hook seam** ([`pkg/op/hooks.go`](../../pkg/op/hooks.go)), where
execution boundaries become observable (`LifecycleHook`: node / subgraph start + complete, carrying the runtime
environment, unit id, result, error). The event stream and narration are two orthogonal projections of that seam;
commands are an inbound channel whose *effects* surface at the same seam.

### Integration status — a refactor, not a green field

Both the event stream and the hook seam pre-date the control plane, and the first cut grew past both:

- **The event-stream spec exists** — `6-execution-topology.md` §Telemetry: `Event{ts, node_id, status, msg}`, a
  `SubscriptionManager` whose `Publish` is non-blocking / drop-on-slow-client (the semantics Slice A's `emit`
  reinvented), the three event categories, ndjson-over-SSH, and the elevation-and-eventing rule (initialize the event
  sinks **before** privilege drop).
- **The hook seam exists** — `pkg/op/hooks.go` (`LifecycleHook` / `HookRegistry`), from
  [`orchestration-primitives.md` §Step 7](../plans/orchestration-primitives.md). Two drifts to reconcile: the interface
  specified `OnPhaseStart` / `OnPhaseComplete` but ships `OnSubgraphStart` / `OnSubgraphComplete` (so run-status
  transitions have no callback — the reason Slice A emits them inline), and no production consumer registers a hook.
- **Slice A fused and duplicated** — it put `Request` / response **and** `Subscribe` / `emit` on one `ControlPlane` and
  emitted events via bespoke inline calls beside each `Fire*` (e.g. `pkg/op/node.go:160`–`161`) instead of through a
  hook. The integration: split the event stream off the command surface, build it on the §Telemetry spec, feed it from a
  `LifecycleHook`, and leave narration orthogonal.

**Terminology.** `Observation` is a settled domain term — a point-in-time **resource fact** (`op.ObservationBase`, the
result of an observe action; [`4-resource-management.md`](4-resource-management.md) §3, observations). It is **not** the event
stream: observations flow *through* events as results, and the outbound structured channel is **events**, never
"observations."

## Thesis (command + event design rationale — see [Framing](#framing-three-orthogonal-pieces))

A run is **steered** from outside and **observed** from outside. Those are one concern, not two, and they belong on
one object: the **control plane**, a channel with two directions. *(Superseded 2026-07-21 — "observed" splits into two
orthogonal channels, events and narration, neither owned by the plane; the async, head-of-line-free command + event
design below still governs the command channel and the event push.)*

- **Commands in** — a consumer *steers* the run: `pause`, `stop`, `step`.
- **Events out** — a consumer *observes* the run: phase transitions, errors, results.

The model is the **Cassandra native protocol**, not a plain request/response RPC. A pure RPC (e.g. the Cosmos DB
direct/RNTBD protocol) carries only request frames and their responses, and pays head-of-line blocking to multiplex
responses down one ordered conduit. The Cassandra channel adds a **second axis** — unsolicited **server push** (its
`REGISTER` → `EVENT` frames, which answer no request) — on top of stream-id-correlated request/response. The control
plane is that richer channel: **`Request → response`** *and* **`Subscribe → events`**.

The plane is **fully async** and **transport-agnostic**. Its core is in-process Go channels; a wire listener (HTTP/2)
bridges a remote consumer to it — and because the bridge is a separate layer, the transport choice never leaks into
the core.

## The core — an in-process async `ControlPlane`

Go's concurrency is goroutines + channels + `select`. The idiom is *"blocking is free"* — a parked goroutine costs a
few KB and zero CPU — so an async **surface** is built by **returning channels**: the channel *is* the future, and the
caller is never forced to block (it `<-`s to await, `select`s to poll, or `select`s over many to multiplex — `select`
is the event loop). Nothing in the plane's API blocks.

```go
type ControlCommand int32

const (
	ControlPause ControlCommand = iota // halt at the next control-point; preserve for resume
	ControlStop                        // halt, unwind (compensate completed work), terminate; not resumable
	ControlStep                        // advance one unit, then re-pause
)

type ControlResponse struct {
	Status RunStatus // the run status the command produced (e.g. phase=paused)
	Err    error     // non-nil when the command was rejected (e.g. pause on a completed run)
}

type ControlEventKind int

const (
	EventPhaseChanged ControlEventKind = iota // a RunStatus transition
	EventError                                // a unit or run error surfaced
	EventResult                               // a terminal result is available
)

type ControlEvent struct {
	Kind   ControlEventKind
	Status RunStatus // the run status at the moment of the event
	UnitID string    // the unit involved, when relevant
	Err    error     // set for EventError
}

// ControlPlane is the run's bidirectional messaging channel. One per run, shared to child executors like the runtime
// environment. Requests in (each correlated to its own response channel), events out (pushed to subscribers).
type ControlPlane struct { /* requests chan; subscriber fan-out; ... */ }

// Request enqueues a command and returns its response channel — the future / stream-id correlation. The executor
// serves it at its next control-point and sends exactly one ControlResponse. Never blocks.
func (p *ControlPlane) Request(cmd ControlCommand) <-chan ControlResponse

// Subscribe returns the pushed event stream and an unsubscribe func (REGISTER → EVENT). Bounded buffer; a slow
// subscriber drops-oldest with a gap signal rather than stalling the run. Never blocks.
func (p *ControlPlane) Subscribe() (events <-chan ControlEvent, cancel func())
```

A consumer writes its own `select` loop — the event loop, nothing hidden:

```go
paused := plane.Request(ControlPause)              // returns instantly
events, unsub := plane.Subscribe(); defer unsub()
for {
	select {
	case r := <-paused: // pause acknowledged: r.Status.Phase == PhasePaused
	case e := <-events: // pushed phase change / error / result
	case <-ctx.Done():  return
	}
}
```

### Why this shape avoids the head-of-line trap

- **No response HOL.** Each `Request` gets its *own* response channel, delivered by an independent goroutine — there is
  no shared ordered response queue for a slow response to block behind. (Response HOL only re-enters at the wire, under
  TCP; see [The wire](#the-wire--an-http2-listener).)
- **Ordering kept only where it means correctness.** Inbound commands are drained in arrival order at the executor's
  **control-point** — a total order at a *safe* point, so `pause`-then-`stop` mean what they say. Queue depth is ~zero;
  it is an ordering guarantee, not a throughput conduit, so it carries no HOL cost.

### Ownership and the context-object taxonomy

The plane lives on the **`GraphExecutor`** — the run's driver and command target — and is shared down to child
executors via `newChildExecutor`, exactly as the runtime environment is. It does **not** belong on `RuntimeEnvironment`
(the world the execution acts on, not the steering of it). The per-run context objects then separate cleanly:

| Object | Concern | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| `context.Context` | cancellation + deadline | external → in: one-shot hard abort |
| `RuntimeEnvironment` | the **world** (catalog, providers, root, resolver) | the substrate acted **on** |
| `ControlPlane` | **commands in** + **events out** | bidirectional: consumer ↔ run |
| `ActivationRecord` | the **one call** (unit, slots, stack) | per-dispatch bundle |

## Commands and the control-point

The pause-flag test becomes a control-point **`switch`** the executor polls at each safe point:

```go
select {
case req := <-e.control.requests: // non-blocking drain
	switch req.cmd {
	case ControlPause:
		e.status = RunStatus{Phase: PhasePaused, ...}
		req.respond(ControlResponse{Status: e.status})
		return ErrPaused  // preserve the recovery stack; resumable via ResumeExecutor
	case ControlStop:
		e.status = RunStatus{Phase: PhaseStopping, Reason: ReasonStopped, ...}
		req.respond(ControlResponse{Status: e.status})
		return ErrStopped // Run unwinds (compensates completed work) + terminates PhaseStopped; not resumable
	}
default: // no command pending → dispatch the next unit
}
```

| | Pause | Stop |
|---|---|---|
| Recovery stack | **preserved** as the resume point | **unwound** (compensate completed work) |
| Terminal phase | `PhasePaused` | `PhaseStopped` |
| Resumable | yes (`ResumeExecutor`) | no |
| `Run` returns | `ErrPaused` (no unwind) | `ErrStopped` (`Run` unwinds, terminates) |

Each new verb is one enum value and one `case` — no new flag, no signature change.

## Events

The executor emits a `ControlEvent` at each lifecycle transition — the `HookRegistry` lifecycle fires
(`FireNodeStart`, `FireSubgraphComplete`, …) become `EventPhaseChanged` / `EventError` emissions on the plane, and
subscribers receive them. Emission is a **non-blocking** send per subscriber; a subscriber that cannot keep up
drops-oldest and is told it missed events (a gap signal), the way a driver drops server events a slow client cannot
absorb — the run is never stalled by an observer.

> **Reframed (2026-07-21).** These events belong to the orthogonal **event stream**
> ([§Telemetry](6-execution-topology.md#telemetry-asynchronous-event-pipeline)), not the command plane, and should be
> produced **through** a `LifecycleHook` rather than the bespoke inline `emit` calls the first cut placed beside each
> `Fire*` (`pkg/op/node.go:161`). Feeding them from hooks also needs the interface's missing run-status-transition
> callback — it ships `OnSubgraph*` where the spec had `OnPhase*`.

## The wire — the broker's HTTP/2 listener

The core plane is in-process channels; a **listener bridges a remote connection to it** — and that listener, revised
2026-10-21, is the broker. It listens on a Unix domain socket (`unix:///var/run/devlore/broker.sock`, illustrative) or
a Windows named pipe (`\\.\pipe\devlore`, illustrative) for local callers, and on TLS for remote ones. From the socket
it reads the peer's uid (`SO_PEERCRED` / `getpeereid`; a named pipe's client token on Windows), which is the local
caller's identity. A *named pipe* on Unix is a FIFO, one-directional and credential-less, which is why the local
transport there is a Unix domain socket. The transport is **HTTP/2**:

- Go's standard library speaks HTTP/1.1 and **HTTP/2** natively and maturely (`net/http` + `golang.org/x/net/http2`),
  but has **no native HTTP/3** — QUIC is experimental in `golang.org/x/net/quic`, and production HTTP/3 means the
  third-party `quic-go` stack. gRPC-Go itself rides HTTP/2.
- A control channel is **low-volume, ordering-sensitive, reliability-first** — the opposite of HTTP/3's target. Its
  headline win (avoiding TCP head-of-line blocking under packet loss) never bites a handful of `pause`/`stop` frames
  and a modest event stream; its other wins (0-RTT, connection migration) are mobile/edge concerns. A control plane
  actually *wants* HTTP/2's **ordered** multiplexing — the very thing HTTP/3 relaxes — because commands need a total
  order.

The transport is a **swappable layer**: if stdlib HTTP/3 ever lands, it is an adapter change, not a plane change.

### The client surface (curl-native)

The listener exposes the plane as **REST commands + Server-Sent Events**, so the whole channel is drivable with `curl`:

- `POST /v1/runs/{runID}/commands` — body `{"command": "pause"|"stop"|"step"}`; the JSON response body **is** the
  response frame (the resulting `RunStatus`, or an error). Request/response.
- `GET  /v1/runs/{runID}/events` — a `text/event-stream` (SSE) the run pushes onto (`REGISTER` → `EVENT`).
- `GET  /v1/runs/{runID}` — the current `RunStatus` (a plain poll, for clients that do not stream).

The typed programmatic API is the gRPC equivalent over the same HTTP/2 connection — a unary `Command` RPC and a
server-streaming `Events` RPC — bridging the **same** in-process `ControlPlane`. curl talks to the REST/SSE facade; a
Go or Java client library talks gRPC. Neither is privileged; both are listeners on the one plane.

### The connection model

The plane is addressed by **run id**, not by connection or session — so the two axes are independent:

- **Different connections.** Subscribe with `GET …/events` on one connection and command with `POST …/commands` on
  another; both resolve to the same run's `ControlPlane` by id. No session state binds events to the connection that
  will command, so a read-only monitor can watch while an operator steers from elsewhere, and many subscribers can
  observe one run.
- **Same connection.** HTTP/2 multiplexes **streams**, not connections: the held-open SSE `GET`, a *second* event
  subscription, and every command `POST` are each an independent stream on the **one** connection — opening another
  event stream or issuing a `pause` is a new *stream*, never a new *connection*. (This is why HTTP/2 earns its place:
  on HTTP/1.1 a streaming `GET` monopolizes the whole connection, forcing a second one per concurrent request —
  HTTP/2 makes same-connection possible, run-id addressing makes multi-connection optional.)

A command never travels **up** the event stream. **SSE is half-duplex** — server → client only — so subscribing to
events opens no upstream channel; a `pause` is always a separate `POST` (its own HTTP/2 stream), whether multiplexed
onto the SSE connection or split onto another. You never open a *second event stream* to issue a command. The **gRPC**
surface instead collapses both directions into one **full-duplex** bidirectional stream — command messages up, event
messages down on a single stream — the closest analog to the Cassandra connection; the REST/SSE surface is the
half-duplex projection of that same plane, traded for being curl-drivable.

This is a deliberate divergence from Cassandra, where `REGISTER` binds events to the connection they return on. Here
events are run-id-bound, not connection-bound; the wire listener is a stateless run-id → plane router, and the
in-process core already supports it — `Subscribe()` fans out to many subscribers, `Request()` takes commands from any
goroutine.

### The runner selector: a plug point

Ruled 2026-10-21: "The control plane looks to see whether it needs to start a new command runner or use an existing
command runner. this is a plug point." An exec names an **account** (root, the caller's own, a service account; there
is always one — [`6.2`](6.2-process-pool.md) § 4). The selector maps `account → runner session`, and *which* runner,
and whether to start one, is its policy:

| Policy | What it does | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| reuse by account | one runner per account, found by its socket; started by the platform (a system daemon for root, a user agent for the caller) | the default on a configured host |
| spawn on demand | fork, `setuid(account)`, exec the runner, a socketpair to it; retired when idle | a service account with no daemon of its own; **root broker only** |
| capped pool | as reuse, with a ceiling on runners and a queue | many accounts, bounded hosts |
| remote host | the account's runner on another machine, reached through that machine's broker | the distributed model ([`1`](1-system-model.md) § 6.2) |

The selector returns a session the broker holds for the run's life and releases at its end; the session is the lease
the authz grant names ([`6.1-authorization.md`](6.1-authorization.md)). The runner knows nothing of the caller.

### Dispatch and the fork

Ruled 2026-10-21: "A single request results in many events: narrations, telemetry, and outputs. Telemetry forks one way
(to a logger). narrations and outputs fork another way (back to the control plane's caller)."

1. The caller: `POST /v1/exec` with the run id, the unit id, the account, the argv or the script with its arguments
   ([`6.3`](6.3-command-execution.md): never a string), the environment, the working directory, and **stdin as an
   attribute** (a byte string, or `auto_confirm`). The response is `202 {exec_id}` and nothing more on that stream.
2. The broker: authenticate, authorize the account, select the runner. It records `exec_id → run, unit`, which is the
   whole routing table for the return path.
3. The broker → the runner: `POST /exec` over its persistent, multiplexed connection to that runner; **the runner's
   response body for that request is the exec's frame stream** until exit. One HTTP/2 stream per exec on the runner
   hop; the same frames, without the facade, when the runner was spawned on demand over a socketpair.
4. The runner ([`6.2`](6.2-process-pool.md)): a fresh child; its stdout and stderr pumped unconditionally into per-exec
   spools so the child never blocks on a slow consumer; frames `{exec_id, channel, payload}` with `channel` one of
   `output`, `narration`, `event`, `control`; `event` also from fd 3 when the child is devlore-aware.
5. **The fork**, in the broker — one goroutine per exec reading the runner's response body, a `switch` on the tag,
   two destinations:
   - `event` → the **bus** ([`2.8`](2.8-eventing-infrastructure.md)); the logger is its subscriber (the run's events
     file, the OpenTelemetry exporter), and `GET /v1/runs/{id}/events` serves any other subscriber. The caller is not
     one unless it subscribes.
   - `narration`, `output`, `control` → the caller's **console stream**, `GET /v1/runs/{id}/console`. The exit status
     rides it as `control`, because the caller is who waits for it.

   The hook seam ([`pkg/op/hooks.go`](../../pkg/op/hooks.go)) feeds the same bus from inside the broker — unit
   scheduled, started, completed, status transitions — so telemetry is the union of what the runner reports about the
   exec and what the executor reports about the unit. The runner does not know there are two destinations; the caller
   does not know there is a bus.

### The two streams: events and console

Each run has two held-open streams, each with its **own `seq`**, because they have different consumers and reconnect
independently; they correlate by exec id, unit id and timestamp. Total order holds within each — across every command's
output and narration on the console, which pipes never gave, and across the run's operational events on the events
stream — and none is needed between them.

| | `GET /v1/runs/{id}/events` | `GET /v1/runs/{id}/console` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| carries | `event:` kinds of [§ Events](#events) and operational events from the runner and fd 3 | `narration`, `output`, `control` |
| for | the logger, monitors, outside processes that watch | the caller (writ) |
| rendered by | the Collector's routing, OpenTelemetry | `narration`'s bytes written into the caller's narration sink as they arrive, under the caller's own `--silent`; `output` into the unit's result |
| `seq` | per run, on the events stream | per run, on the console stream |

**Buffering, three places, three policies.** In the runner, per exec and per channel: the spool, memory then a file,
never dropped, so the child's pipe is always drained ([`6.2`](6.2-process-pool.md)). In the broker, per run and per
stream: an **append-only file** (`events.ndjson`, `console.ndjson`, under the run's directory in the trace store) that
keeps everything, and a **memory ring** over the most recent frames that serves live subscribers and short reconnects
— the ring drops oldest with the gap visible as a jump in `seq`, the file never drops, and a reconnect asking for a
`seq` older than the ring is served from the file. In the caller: narration unbuffered, its bytes straight into the narration sink, never re-rendered (ruled 2026-10-23);
output accumulated per exec into the unit's result up to a cap, beyond which the result holds a reference and the unit
fetches `GET /v1/runs/{id}/output?exec={exec_id}` as `application/octet-stream` from the run's file when it reads it.

**Resume.** Each SSE event's `id:` field is its `seq`. A reconnecting client sends `Last-Event-ID`, as every SSE client
does unasked, and the broker resumes from the buffer; no `since=` parameter is needed beyond what SSE defines. This is
[`6`](6-execution-topology.md)'s "give me everything since X" and its re-attach to a long run, by the standard's own
mechanism.

### The wire, in detail

Two things are called a stream, and the splitting happens at both. **HTTP/2 streams** are the protocol's: one
connection, many request/response pairs, interleaved and demultiplexed by `net/http` with no code of ours — the
held-open console `GET`, an exec `POST`, a `pause`, a trace pull are four streams on one connection. **Channels** are
ours: inside the one console response, every SSE event carries a frame, and SSE's three fields map onto it exactly —
`event:` is the channel, `id:` is `seq`, `data:` is the payload:

```
event: narration
id: 1041
data: {"exec":"e-41","unit":"u-12","len":26,"bytes":"SW5zdGFsbGluZyBvcGVuc3NoLXNlcnZlcgo="}

event: output
id: 1042
data: {"exec":"e-41","len":4096,"bytes":"<base64>"}

event: output
id: 1043
data: {"exec":"e-42","len":210,"bytes":"<base64>"}

event: control
id: 1044
data: {"exec":"e-41","exit_status":0}
```

The caller reads in order and dispatches on `event:`; within a channel, `exec` separates concurrent commands. SSE is
text, hence base64 for output bytes and the octet-stream endpoint for anything large. One stream for all channels,
rather than one per channel, is deliberate: separate HTTP/2 streams have no relative order, and the point of `seq` is a
total order across a run's outputs, narration and events. A consumer that wants one channel asks the broker to filter
(`?channel=narration`) and still receives `seq` with visible gaps.

**Live stdin** has no REST channel; SSE is half-duplex. Three options: stdin as an attribute of the request (a byte
string or `auto_confirm`, which is what `pkg/platform`'s `y\n` stream is today, and covers every package manager);
live stdin on the gRPC or WebSocket full-duplex surface only; or an interactive command refused with a named failure,
as a headless elevation is. **Recommended:** the first by default, the second where a client library asks for it.

### Authorization at the socket

The broker is where authorization is **enforced**; the `authz` provider in the caller's process is where it is
**requested** ([`6.1-authorization.md`](6.1-authorization.md)). Locally the identity is the peer's uid; remotely it is
the token the remote leg presents, minted by an identity-assumption broker — the broker's own API is substrate 2's first
consumer. The policy is the broker's own profile: which peers may ask for which accounts (`offers` and `brokers` in the
`authz` section, resolved for the broker's profile rather than the caller's). A refusal is a **named policy failure**
on the response to `POST /v1/exec`, never a dialog nobody can see ([`6.2`](6.2-process-pool.md) § 10); it reaches the
caller's unit as the error its `ErrorAction` handles. Every grant and every release is an audit record on the run's
events stream and in its file. The broker enforces with what it is told and what the operating system tells it; it
holds no password, no key and no token of its own (ruled 2026-10-22), so a compromised broker can ask runners for
things and nothing else.

### The logon pipeline

App ⇒ broker ⇒ subgraph-executor, sourced from a logon subgraph (§ Ruled 2026-10-23 and 2026-10-24, 1 to 4). The request is the account, the host and the subgraph, with the app's narration flags, so the executor's `--silent` and `-v` are the app's. The broker keys its processors by host and account and checks the executor's version when it connects. While the subgraph runs, the executor's stderr streams to the app as narration frames of bytes; when it ends, its stdout, the result written once through its result pipeline as JSON, returns with the exit status, and the app decodes it into the logon unit's output. The app's own `-o` shapes whatever the final product is. Because subgraphs run in parallel on one executor, every per-run piece of the executor's state is keyed by run ([`6.2`](6.2-process-pool.md)).

### The remote leg, and the direct path: the posted unit

**Ruled 2026-10-24: the remote leg is SSH.** The broker reaches another machine through SSH and ships it a bundle: the executor, installed if absent, so any device SSH reaches can run a logon subgraph; the subgraph; and what it needs to run under the account's credentials. No broker is installed on the far machine. The connection is configured by the user's `~/.ssh/config`. The HTTP/2-over-TLS remote leg and its token, ruled 2026-10-21 and 2026-10-22, are withdrawn; the local leg, HTTP/2 over a Unix socket or named pipe, stands, subject to its confirmation in § Open. [`6`](6-execution-topology.md)'s SSH design returns in this form, the bundle in place of its upload of a runner binary, and the argv rule and the account in place of its `Execute(cmd string, elevated bool)`. The direct path is the other end of the scale: a caller on the runner standing for
its own account, no broker between, the same frames, and the caller stamping `seq` itself.

**The posted unit** (ruled 2026-10-22; the mechanism is `plan.logon`, [`2.3`](2.3-orchestration-primitives.md)):

1. **A remoted unit is a full graph on the wire.** The combinator's body, a subgraph, is re-rooted as an `op.Graph`:
   every binding whose producer lies outside the body is resolved to a literal typed slot, the catalog slice the
   body's resources reference travels with it, and the coordinator signs it. The broker verifies the signature
   before it runs anything ([`5`](5-graph-trace-integrity.md) tier 2), which is § Open 15's answer for a posted
   graph. The two serializations that exist are the only two used: the graph document out, the trace back.
2. **What comes back is the unit's stamped stack** (proposed 2026-10-22; how the stack and the trace travel is open since 2026-10-23, § Open), the same `{unit_id, result, result_type, status, entries}` tree
   the trace already serializes, on the last control frame of the console stream. The coordinator pushes it nested
   onto the parent stack as it would any subgraph's.
3. **The remote is stateless between calls; the trace is the state.** Undo is the stamped stack posted back to the
   same broker for a state-checked unwind, `ResumeUnwind`, where the effects are: the coordinator's nested entry is a
   proxy compensator whose `Compensate` does that post. Resume is graph plus partial trace posted again, and the
   remote runs `ResumeExecutor`. The broker keeps its per-run events and console files for observation and nothing
   else.
4. **The remote executor is a saga boundary.** It exhausts its own retry policy and unwinds to its entry
   precondition before it reports failure, so the coordinator receives a unit that is done, or failed and already
   undone, or `stopped × compensation_failed` with the stack that says so.
5. **Exit is the run-state machine's terminal at the cut** ([`2.2`](2.2-phase-execution.md)): `completed`,
   `stopped` after the unwind, or `paused` with the trace. The remote never waits on the coordinator; a slow or
   absent one is the spool's problem ([`6.2`](6.2-process-pool.md) § 4.1). A `Stop` at the coordinator is forwarded
   as this plane's command to the remote run id, which unwinds its boundary; the coordinator does not end with a
   remote unit still running. A `Pause` leaves a trace and no process on the remote.

### Channels, wire protocol and transport, by hop

Laid out on 2026-10-24, after the rulings of 2026-10-23 and 2026-10-24 landed, as the one place a reader finds what a request uses on every hop.

**The channels.** What flows, logically, independent of the wire:

- **Request, app ⇒ broker.** One of three: an *exec*, one command by account, iteration 1's degenerate case of a logon holding one command; a *post*, a logon subgraph with its account, its host, its graph document and the coordinator's signature, and the app's narration flags; a *command*, `pause`, `stop` or `step`, steering a run by its id.
- **Console, executor ⇒ broker ⇒ app.** One stream per run, with a `seq` of its own:

| Channel | Carries | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `narration` | stderr's bytes, unread | as they are written |
| `output` | stdout's bytes, accumulated into the result | as they are written, decoded at the end |
| `control` | the exit status, an error, and for a posted unit its outcome | once, at the end |

- **Events, from every component**, to the OpenTelemetry Collector, never on the console; anyone subscribes ([`2.8`](2.8-eventing-infrastructure.md)).
- **Input, app ⇒ executor.** Stdin fixed at the start, bytes or auto-confirm; live input is § Open 20.

**The wire protocol.** What the messages look like:

- **Requests** are JSON over HTTP, the same shapes in every iteration: `POST /v1/exec` with an `ExecRequest`, answered `202 {"exec_id"}` or `403` with a named refusal; `POST /v1/post` with a `PostRequest`, the account, the host, the graph as base64 and the signature; `POST /v1/runs/{id}/commands` and `GET /v1/runs/{id}`.
- **The console** is Server-Sent Events on `GET /v1/runs/{id}/console`, one event per frame, `event:` the channel, `id:` the `seq`, `data:` the payload:

```
event: narration
id: 1041
data: {"exec":"e-41","unit":"u-12","len":26,"bytes":"SW5zdGFsbGluZyBvcGVuc3NoLXNlcnZlcgo="}

event: control
id: 1044
data: {"exec":"e-41","exit_status":0}
```

  `output` has the shape of `narration`; `control` is `{"exec","exit_status","error","outcome"}`. Bytes are base64 inside JSON, a third more on the wire; bulk output has the octet-stream endpoint, `GET /v1/runs/{id}/output?exec=…` (§ Open 11). A reconnecting client sends `Last-Event-ID`.
- **The runner hop** carries frames of `{rid, channel, payload}` with the same three channels: the response body of an HTTP/2 stream, or length-prefixed on a socketpair ([`6.2`](6.2-process-pool.md) § 7).
- **Events** are OTLP, OpenTelemetry's protocol, over gRPC on port 4317 or HTTP on 4318: spans, logs and metrics. Devlore defines no event format of its own.

**The transport.** What carries each hop, in each iteration:

| Hop | Iteration 1 (now) | Iteration 2 | Iteration 3, local | Iteration 3, remote |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| app ⇄ broker | Go channels, in-process | HTTP/2 cleartext over a Unix socket or named pipe (§ Open 19) | the same | the same |
| broker ⇄ executor | fork and exec in-process, `sudo -n` for root | the same | frames over a socket, pipe or socketpair | SSH |
| any ⇄ Collector | OTLP | OTLP | OTLP | OTLP over SSH (§ Open 17) |

**SSH, the remote hop.** One SSH session per posted subgraph: its stdin delivers the bundle and the graph; its stderr is narration, which the broker reframes as `narration` frames with `seq` for the app; its stdout is the result; its exit status is the run's; cancel is SSH's signal request, and failing that, closing the channel. A session carries file descriptors 0, 1 and 2 only, so events cross by a channel of devlore's own on the same connection, with Go's SSH package, or by a remote port forward, `ssh -R 4317:localhost:4317`, with the OpenSSH client (§ Open 17). Whether one connection carries many sessions, and what runs on the far side, are § Open 21 and 22.

### Message reference

Three message shapes cross the wire. `command` is the only required request field; `request_id` is an optional client
correlation token (redundant over HTTP's own request/response pairing, load-bearing on the async gRPC stream); `seq`
is a per-run monotonic counter on every event, so a subscriber detects the gap when the bounded buffer drops-oldest.

**Request** — `POST /v1/runs/{runID}/commands`:

```json
{ "command": "pause" }
{ "command": "stop" }
{ "command": "step", "count": 1 }             // count optional, default 1
{ "command": "pause", "request_id": "c-17" }  // request_id optional on any command
```

**Response** — the POST's JSON body:

```json
// accepted (HTTP 200)
{ "status": { "phase": "paused", "condition": "healthy", "reason": "paused" }, "request_id": "c-17" }

// rejected (HTTP 409) — the error half of the response frame, never a silent no-op
{ "error": "cannot pause: run already completed", "request_id": "c-17" }
```

**Exec** — `POST /v1/exec` (revised 2026-10-21; the response is `202` with the exec id, and everything else arrives on
the console stream):

```json
{ "run": "run-7f3a", "unit": "u-12", "account": "root",
  "argv": ["apt-get", "install", "-y", "openssh-server"],
  "env": { "DEBIAN_FRONTEND": "noninteractive" }, "cwd": "/", "stdin": "auto_confirm" }

{ "run": "run-7f3a", "unit": "u-13", "account": "caller",
  "script": { "dialect": "sh", "body": "set -eu\njournalctl -u \"$1\" --since \"$2\" | tail -20" },
  "args": ["sshd", "-1h"] }
```

```json
// accepted (HTTP 202)
{ "exec_id": "e-41" }

// refused by policy (HTTP 403) — a named failure, never a dialog
{ "error": "account root is not offered to uid 501 by this broker", "offer": "off_host_root" }
```

**Event** — an SSE frame on `GET /v1/runs/{runID}/events` (`event:` names the kind, `data:` is JSON carrying `seq`):

```
event: phase_changed
data: {"seq":42,"phase":"running","condition":"healthy","unit":"file.mkdir-1"}

event: error
data: {"seq":43,"phase":"stopping","condition":"execution_failed","unit":"file.move-3","message":"source not found"}

event: result
data: {"seq":44,"phase":"completed","condition":"healthy","result":{"...":"..."}}
```

## Client examples — `curl`

Subscribe to the event stream in one terminal (`-N` streams live, no buffering — this is the pushed `EVENT` axis):

```console
$ curl -sSN http://localhost:8080/v1/runs/run-7f3a/events
event: phase_changed
data: {"seq":1,"phase":"running","condition":"healthy","unit":"file.mkdir-1"}

event: phase_changed
data: {"seq":2,"phase":"running","condition":"healthy","unit":"file.write_text-2"}
```

Steer the run from another terminal. The `POST` is the request; the JSON body it returns **is** the response frame (the
ack — the `RunStatus` the command produced):

```console
$ curl -sS -X POST http://localhost:8080/v1/runs/run-7f3a/commands \
       -H 'content-type: application/json' -d '{"command":"pause","request_id":"c-17"}'
{"status":{"phase":"paused","condition":"healthy","reason":"paused"},"request_id":"c-17"}
```

…and the first terminal receives the pushed event for the same transition:

```
event: phase_changed
data: {"seq":3,"phase":"paused","condition":"healthy","reason":"paused"}
```

Resume is a separate lifecycle call (`ResumeExecutor`), but `stop` ends the run here and now — it unwinds
(compensating completed work) and terminates, and is **not** resumable:

```console
$ curl -sS -X POST http://localhost:8080/v1/runs/run-7f3a/commands \
       -H 'content-type: application/json' -d '{"command":"stop"}'
{"status":{"phase":"stopping","condition":"healthy","reason":"stopped"}}
```

The event stream then shows the halt reaching its terminal. A clean operator stop is **not** a failure — it lands
`stopped × healthy × stopped` (a failed unwind would instead land `stopped × compensation_failed`):

```
event: phase_changed
data: {"seq":4,"phase":"stopping","condition":"healthy","reason":"stopped"}

event: phase_changed
data: {"seq":5,"phase":"stopped","condition":"healthy","reason":"stopped"}
```

A one-shot status poll, for a client that does not want to hold the stream open:

```console
$ curl -sS http://localhost:8080/v1/runs/run-7f3a
{"phase":"stopped","condition":"healthy","reason":"stopped"}
```

Rejected commands come back as the error half of the response frame (HTTP `409`), never a silent no-op:

```console
$ curl -sS -o /dev/null -w '%{http_code}\n' -X POST \
       http://localhost:8080/v1/runs/run-7f3a/commands \
       -H 'content-type: application/json' -d '{"command":"pause"}'
409
```

## Relationship to the rest of the model

- **[2.2 Phase execution](2.2-phase-execution.md)** owns the `RunStatus` machine (`preparing` / `running` / `paused` /
  `stopping` / `stopped` / `completed`); the control plane is its external driver and observer — commands move phases,
  events report them.
- **[2.6 Execution policies](2.6-execution-policies.md)** owns retry / transition reactions; a policy-driven `stop`
  (a `ReactionStop`) and an operator `ControlStop` reach the same terminal by different triggers.
- **Narration (`status.Narrator`) and the event stream are orthogonal to the plane** —
  see [Framing](#framing-three-orthogonal-pieces). They do **not** migrate onto the plane as event kinds (the earlier
  plan). Narration stays a per-process human channel; the structured events are the
  [§Telemetry](6-execution-topology.md#telemetry-asynchronous-event-pipeline) pipeline, fed from the
  [`pkg/op/hooks.go`](../../pkg/op/hooks.go) seam. `Result` (`result.Pipeline`) likewise stays its own output channel; an
  observe action's `Observation` rides the event stream as a result, not as a plane-owned kind.
- **[6 Execution topology](6-execution-topology.md) §Telemetry** owns the event-stream spec (`Event`,
  `SubscriptionManager`, categories, ndjson, elevation-safe sinks); **[`pkg/op/hooks.go`](../../pkg/op/hooks.go)** is the
  shared emission seam for both the event stream and narration.
- **[6.2 The command runner](6.2-process-pool.md)** owns the runner daemons the selector hands out and the spools behind
  the frames; **[6.1 Authorization](6.1-authorization.md)** owns the provider that requests what this broker enforces;
  **[2.8 Eventing](2.8-eventing-infrastructure.md)** owns the bus the fork's `event` branch feeds and the audience rule
  for diagnostics.

## Open

Named here so a reader can tell a ruling from a proposal. Each is the owner's to rule; until then the document records
the question.

1. **Command runner or graph executor — ruled 2026-10-22: the graph executor, on the runner.** "Option 2 is the way
   to go." The second contains the first: a command is one kind of node in a posted unit, and a unit of one command
   node is the command runner's case. writ's System graph reaches root as `plan.logon("root", …)`, its file
   operations running as themselves. The protocol is § The remote leg; the control structure is
   [`2.3`](2.3-orchestration-primitives.md) § Ruled 2026-10-22.
2. **One binary or two:** `devlore broker` in the one binary, or its own.
3. **Installation and the first elevation — narrowed 2026-10-22.** The broker is unprivileged and is an ordinary
   service. What is installed once under elevation is the **runners**, one service per account the machine offers
   ([`6.2`](6.2-process-pool.md) § 4.1), and that registration is the one elevation that cannot go through the
   broker. Open: who registers a runner for a new account, writ or lore, and what a machine with no broker does.
4. **The local policy's source:** the peer uid's group, a broker-side allow list, or the `authz` offers resolved in
   the broker's own profile; and whether offers and brokers are configured in the caller, the broker, or both.
   Whatever the source, it names accounts and peers, never a credential: the broker holds none (ruled 2026-10-22).
5. **Remote authorization — answered 2026-10-24.** SSH authenticates the connection by the user's keys and configuration; the far side's broker elevates to the logon's account by its own privileges. Tokens and mTLS are withdrawn with the TLS remote leg.
6. **A per-user agent beside the system broker,** for the caller's own account and interpreter state.
7. **Session and lifetime:** idle timeout; what a crashed caller's runners do; re-attach after a restart.
8. **Version skew** between a client and its broker, and the API's versioning. Narrowed 2026-10-24: the broker supplies the executor at the app's version, locally by reuse or start, remotely in the bundle, and checks it when it connects.
9. **Where traces are written** when a remote broker executes: its store, collected by the coordinator.
10. **Narration frames — answered 2026-10-23: neither.** A frame carries what the program wrote to stderr, as bytes, and the caller writes them into its narration sink unaltered.
11. **Binary output:** base64 inline below a size and the octet-stream endpoint above it, or always the endpoint.
12. **The gRPC twin** in the first cut, or the REST facade alone, since Go clients are the only typed clients today.
13. **The per-run ring under a chatty command:** drop oldest, or block the runner's drain.
14. **Cross-run subscriptions** with a filter, for an outside process that watches every run on a machine or every
    failure of a unit — today's subscriptions are per run id.
15. **Authorization of an outside actor** that commands a run; the control plane predates `authz`, and the broker is
    where the check lives. For a *posted graph* the answer is the coordinator's signature, verified before the run
    (§ The remote leg, ruled 2026-10-22); for a command on a run, still open.
16. **Where a logon subgraph's trace and recovery stack live**, locally and remotely: centralized or distributed. The trace must be part of the record on the app's side and also available to the executor, with the recovery site, which lives inside the run's authority boundary on the machine where the effect happened ([`5.3`](5.3-recovery-site.md)). #1158.
17. **The shipment back over SSH, and the remote events channel.** SSH carries file descriptors 0, 1 and 2. Recommended: Go's SSH package, whose one connection carries a channel of our own for OpenTelemetry beside the session on every platform; the alternative with the OpenSSH client is a remote port forward to the Collector, `ssh -R 4317:localhost:4317`. #1160.
18. **The SSH client.** Go's `x/crypto/ssh`, reading a host's resolved configuration from `ssh -G`, with `x/crypto/ssh/agent` and `x/crypto/ssh/knownhosts`, or the system's OpenSSH client, which honours the whole configuration but multiplexes nowhere on Windows.
19. **The local leg.** This document reads the ruling of 2026-10-24 as withdrawing the HTTP/2-over-TLS remote leg and keeping HTTP/2 over a Unix socket or named pipe locally. To confirm.
20. **Input from the user while a command runs**, in three kinds: keystrokes forwarded to a program's stdin, the console stream in reverse; a pseudo-terminal for a program that wants one, which merges stdout and stderr and so is ruled out where stdout is a result; and a structured question from devlore's own programs, relayed by the broker and asked through the app's Narrator, where `--unattended` answers with the default. Recommended: the third first.
21. **One SSH connection per processor, or one per subgraph?** Ruling 9 runs many subgraphs in parallel on one elevated executor, named by host and account. Over SSH that holds only if one connection carries many sessions: native in Go's SSH package; with the OpenSSH client it needs connection multiplexing, which Windows lacks, and otherwise every subgraph costs a connection, a login and an elevation of its own. Recommended: this is the deciding factor of § Open 18, in favour of Go's SSH package. For ruling.
22. **On the far side, an executor process per SSH session, or a daemon the sessions reach?** If each session starts its own executor, there is no long-lived processor to reuse remotely, and each subgraph pays a fresh process and a fresh elevation. If the bundle installs a long-lived executor that sessions connect to, ruling 9 holds remotely, but devlore leaves a daemon running on machines it touches, close to the broker everywhere the owner ruled out. Recommended: a process per session first, which is simpler and leaves nothing behind, with reuse a local optimisation until a remote host needs it. For ruling.
